About

Farhia Dini is a Somali economist, lecturer, and researcher whose work focuses on development economics, economic empowerment, research-informed teaching, and institutional innovation. She is committed to building practical pathways for student success, women-led entrepreneurship, and evidence-based policy engagement in Somalia.

Academic Interests

Development Economics Applied Econometrics Climate Economics FDI Food Security Digital Transformation Women’s Economic Empowerment

Professional Focus

My work bridges higher education, employability, innovation, and economic inclusion. I am especially interested in designing programs that strengthen career readiness, support women and girls, expand access to digital tools, and turn academic knowledge into practical community outcomes.

Research

My research engages with economic development challenges in fragile and emerging contexts, with a growing focus on policy relevance, institutional transformation, and inclusive growth.

Core Areas

Applied economics, econometrics, infrastructure and growth, renewable energy, climate economics, food security, and development policy in Sub-Saharan Africa and Somalia.

Current Themes

Women’s economic participation, youth employability, digital transformation, entrepreneurship ecosystems, and evidence-based institutional planning in fragile settings.

Research Goal

To produce high-quality research that informs teaching, supports decision-making, and contributes to development debates relevant to Somalia, Africa, and the wider Global South.

Teaching

I teach with a strong emphasis on clarity, applied examples, and practical skills that help students connect theory to real-world economic and social challenges.

Courses & Themes

  • Development Economics
  • Statistics and Applied Quantitative Skills
  • Econometrics and Empirical Analysis
  • Research Methods and Academic Writing
  • Data Analytics for Undergraduate and Graduate Learners

Teaching Philosophy

I value student-centered teaching that is practical, motivating, and academically rigorous. My approach encourages critical thinking, evidence use, communication skills, and confidence-building for both classroom success and future careers.
